Hand off vs keep.

You don't hand over everything. You hand over the parts the editor can do faster than you.

Hand off
  • Cuts, pacing, transitions
  • Color grade (D-Log → Rec709 + LUTs)
  • Captions, motion graphics, callouts
  • B-roll selection + placement
  • Sound design + music
  • File delivery (export specs, naming)
Keep
  • Hooks. Always.
  • Voice + POV
  • Strategy + ideation
  • Taste (final approval)
  • Brand voice + style
  • Account management

My actual job post.

Copy it, swap in your details, post on Upwork or Onlinejobs.ph.

Title

Short-Form Video Editor Wanted — Female Founder Building in Public Needs Your Skills

About the Role

We're looking for a short-form video editor to join the [YOUR NAME] team — a fully remote team creating content around [YOUR NICHE]. This is an ongoing role with consistent weekly volume.

About the Channel

[YOUR BIO + REAL NUMBERS]. Content covers [YOUR TOPICS]. The tone is direct, grounded, and transparent — not corporate, not over-polished. Think smart editing that enhances the story without stealing the show.

What You'll Do

Edit short-form vertical videos (Reels, TikToks, Shorts — 15-90 seconds) from raw footage + a detailed brief. Punchy cuts, scroll-stopping hooks, on-screen text with great pacing, b-roll layering, sound design. Match the brand's visual style across every clip. Hit fast turnaround on a steady weekly volume.

What the Briefs Look Like

Our briefs are detailed and shot-by-shot — covering hook, transcript, text overlay specs, b-roll direction, sound effects, brand colors, and asset links. Real example brief here.

Must-Haves

Experience editing short-form vertical content (Reels, TikTok, Shorts). Able to execute from a detailed brief independently. Strong sense of hooks + pacing. Reliable + communicative. Portfolio with vertical edits.

Compensation

$15-$20 per short-form video, based on experience. Long-form compensated separately if it comes up. Fully remote. Ongoing role with consistent weekly volume.

How to Apply

Send: 2-3 examples of short-form vertical videos you've edited (Reels/TikToks/Shorts preferred), a brief note on your editing style, your current turnaround time for a single short, and your preferred editing software (CapCut, Premiere, DaVinci, etc.). We'll set up a short paid test edit for top candidates.

What you're testing
  • Taste — do you like what they shipped?
  • Deadline — did they honor it?
  • Questions — did they ask to understand?
  • Closeness — how near were they to your ideal?

My non-negotiable standards.

Every editor on my team signs these before they start. Print the agreement, have them sign.

  • 5-hour max reply time during working hours
  • 24-hour first cut from brief
  • 24-hour turnaround on revisions
  • 12-hour blocker flag if anything's missing
  • Exact filename + folder conventions
  • Color grade applied (D-Log → Rec709 + LUTs)
  • Captions burned in, manually corrected
  • Hook untouched, B-roll audio muted

How we communicate.

Slack is the hub. Async by default. Real-time when it matters.

  • One assignments channel per editor. Briefs land where they belong. No DM hunting for what's the latest spec.
  • One inbox channel per editor. Questions, blockers, work-in-progress live there. Async-friendly.
  • #ready-to-post is the finish line. Finished cuts posted publicly. Wins are seen, standards stay visible.
  • 5-hour reply standard during working hours. Async doesn't mean silent.

The handoff system.

It's not Drive. It's albums. Way faster than uploading raw footage every time.

  • Create B-roll albums on your iPhone with PixSort.
  • For a new video, drop the relevant clips into one album.
  • Share the album link with your editor.
  • Editor pulls B-roll + returns the cut via Drive or Dropbox.

The brief. Every video starts with one.

If it's not in the brief, the editor's guessing. Same skeleton every time. Below is the simplified version — copy + paste straight into Google Docs.

Editor brief — template
[Project code] · [length] · [style]
Open + copy →

Source clip

Folder: [where the raw lives] · File: [filename] · Link: [iCloud / Drive / Frame.io] · In frame: [who's in the shot] · Note: [anything weird]

Hook (0:00–0:05)

Pull this line from the [start / end / middle] of the clip:

"[The exact opening line — copy/paste from the transcript]"

Hard cut in. No intro, no fade. Let it land — then cut to Beat 1.

Text overlays

Beat Timecode Overlay text Purpose
Hook0:00"[curiosity line]"Stop the scroll
Beat 1~0:05"[intro line]"Establish who / what
Beat 2~0:15"[insight line]"Name the insight before they do
Beat 3~0:32"[payoff setup]"Set up what's about to happen

Style: Top-center, small font, clean. Fade in at beat start, hold ~3 sec. Never overlay during a close-up.

Editing notes

Music: [none under dialogue / soft under B-roll] · Captions: white text, black outline, bottom third · Highlight words: ["X", "Y", "Z"] — bold in captions · Never: [overlay during a close-up / music over the hook] · Tone: [authentic / cinematic / fast-cut]

Export

Aspect: 9:16 (1080×1920) · Filename: [project-code]-[descriptor]-v1.mp4
