The split

Hand off vs keep.

You don't hand over everything. You hand over the parts the editor can do faster than you.

Hand off
  • ✓Cuts, pacing, transitions
  • ✓Color grade (D-Log → Rec709 + LUTs)
  • ✓Captions, motion graphics, callouts
  • ✓B-roll selection + placement
  • ✓Sound design + music
  • ✓File delivery (export specs, naming)
Keep
  • ★Hooks. Always.
  • ★Voice + POV
  • ★Strategy + ideation
  • ★Taste (final approval)
  • ★Brand voice + style
  • ★Account management

Comment under editor-influencer reels. The people commenting are upskilling editors looking for work. Slide into their DMs.

Standards

Set the standard. Hold the line.

Every editor on my team signs this before they start. Non-negotiable.

  • →5-hour max reply time during working hours
  • →24-hour first cut from brief
  • →24-hour turnaround on revisions
  • →12-hour blocker flag if anything's missing
  • →Exact filename + folder conventions
  • →Color grade applied (D-Log → Rec709)
  • →Captions burned in, manually corrected
  • →Hook untouched, B-roll audio muted

Communication

One channel structure scales with every new editor. Everyone knows where to look.

01. One assignments channel per editor. Briefs land where they belong. No DM hunting.
02. One inbox channel per editor. Questions, blockers, work-in-progress live there.
03. #ready-to-post is the finish line. Finished cuts get posted publicly. Wins seen.
04. 5-hour reply standard during working hours. Async doesn't mean silent.

2 years in

What I'd tell 2-years-ago me.

  1. It's not easy. Finding consistent, quality editors takes work. Don't expect to nail it round one.
  2. Hold the standard. The brief is the brief. If you bend, they'll bend back.
  3. Show, don't tell. Examples + guidelines beat verbal direction every time.
  4. Set response times + expectations upfront. Hold them to it from day one.
  5. The first hire is the hardest. Each one after gets faster.
