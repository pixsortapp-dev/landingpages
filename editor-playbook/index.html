Hand off vs keep.

You don't hand over everything. You hand over the parts the editor can do faster than you.

Hand off
  • Cuts, pacing, transitions
  • Color grade (D-Log → Rec709 + LUTs)
  • Captions, motion graphics, callouts
  • B-roll selection + placement
  • Sound design + music
  • File delivery (export specs, naming)
Keep
  • Hooks. Always.
  • Voice + POV
  • Strategy + ideation
  • Taste (final approval)
  • Brand voice + style
  • Account management

My actual job post.

Copy this, swap in your details, post on Upwork or Onlinejobs.ph.

Title

Video Editor Wanted — Female Founder Traveling the World Needs Your Skills

About the Role

We're looking for a video editor to join the [YOUR NAME] YouTube team — a fully remote team creating content around [YOUR NICHE]. This is an ongoing role with consistent work available.

About the Channel

[YOUR BIO + REAL NUMBERS]. Content covers [YOUR TOPICS]. The tone is direct, grounded, and transparent — not corporate, not over-polished. Think smart editing that enhances the story without stealing the show.

What You'll Do

Edit long-form YouTube videos (8-10 min) from raw footage + a detailed scene-by-scene brief. Cut for pacing and clarity. Add b-roll, text overlays, captions, motion graphics, animated callouts per brief. Match the brand's visual style. Edit short-form vertical content as it becomes available. Incorporate feedback efficiently and hit deadlines.

What the Briefs Look Like

Our editor briefs are detailed and scene-by-scene — covering transcript, text overlays, b-roll direction, motion graphics specs, sound effects, brand colors, and asset links. Real example brief here.

Must-Haves

Experience editing YouTube content (talking-head or educational/lifestyle hybrid). Able to execute from a detailed brief independently. Strong sense of pacing. Reliable + communicative. Portfolio links.

Compensation

$30-$60 per long-form video (8-10 min), based on experience. Short-form compensated separately. Fully remote. Ongoing role with consistent volume.

How to Apply

Send: 2-3 examples of YouTube videos you've edited, a brief note on your editing style, your current turnaround time for an 8-10 min video, your preferred editing software. We'll set up a short paid test edit for top candidates.

The paid trial.

Pay for the trial. Free trials get free quality.

Long-form: 1-minute intro edit from your raw footage + brief.

Short-form: Full short-form video, end to end.

What you're testing
  • Taste — do you like what they shipped?
  • Deadline — did they honor it?
  • Questions — did they ask to understand?
  • Closeness — how near were they to your ideal?

My non-negotiable standards.

Every editor on my team signs these before they start. Print the agreement, have them sign.

  • 5-hour max reply time during working hours
  • 24-hour first cut from brief
  • 24-hour turnaround on revisions
  • 12-hour blocker flag if anything's missing
  • Exact filename + folder conventions
  • Color grade applied (D-Log → Rec709 + LUTs)
  • Captions burned in, manually corrected
  • Hook untouched, B-roll audio muted

How we communicate.

Slack is the hub. Async by default. Real-time when it matters.

  • Briefs drop in one channel. No DM confusion about what's the latest spec.
  • Questions go to the PM (Jatin, for us), not me. Frees the founder to film + strategize.
  • Finished cuts get posted publicly. Wins are seen. Standards stay visible.
  • 5-hour reply standard during working hours. Async doesn't mean silent.

The handoff system.

It's not Drive. It's albums. Way faster than uploading raw footage every time.

  • Create B-roll albums on your iPhone with PixSort.
  • For a new video, drop the relevant clips into one album.
  • Share the album link with your editor.
  • Editor pulls B-roll + returns the cut via Drive or Dropbox.
